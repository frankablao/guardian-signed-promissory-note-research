## Electronic student financial administration

Electronic submission does not describe an entire financial workflow. UCLA's published loan instructions describe electronic note signing alongside review and retention of loan forms [9](../references.md#ref-9). A Philippine example, UST's 8 January 2024 enrollment announcement, directs submission of a signed-note copy through Google Forms within a specified window and subject to finance approval [10](../references.md#ref-10). These are documentary examples, not evaluations of processing gains or proof that either workflow matches Mabini's. UCLA's undated page also contains obsolete browser guidance; neither example is used as current operational advice.

In a randomized U.S. FAFSA experiment, Bettinger et al. report increased applications with combined assistance and information, while information alone did not improve outcomes [4](../references.md#ref-4). This concerns an assistance intervention in a different aid system, not an electronic-signature effect. For A-RQ1, the proposed application is to investigate who needs help, what the current steps require, and where guardian access or waiting creates difficulty. For A-RQ2/A-RQ3, it motivates considering help and recovery in the design and walkthroughs. These are researcher's inferences; the paper supplies no Mabini effect size or evidence of repayment, retention, or academic gains.

## Acknowledgment, authentication, and signing

| Concept | Meaning for this study | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electronic acknowledgment and handwritten capture | Review displayed terms, explicitly accept them, and draw a mark with touch or mouse. | Records an acceptance action and captured mark; the mark alone does not establish who drew it. |
| Authenticated student session | Signing occurs within the student's logged-in account, with that context recorded. | Authentication concerns the student account; it is not separate guardian authentication. |
| Independent guardian identity verification | Separate evidence/process would establish confidence in the guardian's claimed identity. | Not supplied by the established shared-session constraint. Guardian accounts, external signing links, OTP, and external signature providers remain excluded under #740. |
| Cryptographic digital signature | Algorithmic signing/verification, distinct from drawing a name. | Not established by storing a signature image and timestamp; not claimed by this design. |

NIST distinguishes identity proofing from control of account-bound authenticators [6](../references.md#ref-6); FIPS 186-5 describes algorithmic digital signatures [7](../references.md#ref-7). Applying these concepts to the student-session constraint [3](../references.md#ref-3) is Study A's reasoning. The U.S. publications provide terminology, not portal certification or a determination of Philippine legal enforceability. A-RQ2 concerns accurate signing explanations and requirements; A-RQ3 should investigate misunderstandings about who is authenticated and what acceptance means. Actual implementation behavior and signing experiences belong in Study B.

## Preserving the signed record

NARA's Bulletin 2015-03 distinguishes business records from records of authentication and connects their management to continuing access and validation [8](../references.md#ref-8). Its scope is U.S. federal recordkeeping. It informs the design rationale for considering accepted terms and their relationship to a signing event; it sets no Mabini retention period and demonstrates no local record integrity.

Under #740, both offices must review the same accepted terms despite later profile or configuration changes, with guardian signing before Dean approval and Dean approval before President's Office approval [3](../references.md#ref-3). That order, cancellation/replacement rules, access restrictions, and legacy handling are **established issue constraints**, not findings derived from NARA or validated local practice. Study A must trace these constraints alongside permitted local evidence, model their interactions, and walk through how participants interpret them. Whether the implemented system enforces preservation, permissions, sequence, and legacy compatibility requires version-linked technical verification in Study B and mc-portal.

## Formative design validation and usability

Brooke's original SUS chapter treats usability as dependent on users, tasks, and context and provides a composite subjective measure following system use [5](../references.md#ref-5). It supports attending to context; it does not validate a mockup, measure payment-term comprehension, or authenticate a signer. A-RQ3 can use role-specific walkthrough tasks and separate prompts about amount, deadline, status, signing meaning, help, and anticipated burden. These prompts are proposed study choices. SUS is not selected or validated here for static mockups; any later questionnaire use, translation, adaptation, and scoring rationale require a documented instrument decision under #5. Implemented-system usability evaluation belongs in Study B.

Gregor and Hevner position design science contributions against problem and solution knowledge [1](../references.md#ref-1). FEDS distinguishes formative/summative purposes and artificial/naturalistic evaluation [2](../references.md#ref-2). These limited claims were verified against original abstracts; inaccessible full texts restrict appraisal. Study A proposes formative walkthroughs of versioned process/state models and synthetic mockups, with feedback and revision decisions, to develop local requirements and a design contribution. It does not need a deployed artifact to plan that work. Framework terminology does not prove that walkthroughs occurred, the design is valid, or operational benefits exist.

## Question mapping and specific local gap

The bounded gap is the evidence needed to justify and formatively validate this proposed workflow in the confirmed participating Mabini units. The literature supplies concepts, methods, and examples; #740 supplies constraints. Neither establishes the current local need, participants' interpretation of the design, or a validated handoff. This is not a first-of-its-kind novelty claim.
